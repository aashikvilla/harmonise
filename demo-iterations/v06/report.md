# Independent cold review — v06

## Executive decision: shortlist

Harmonise earns a shortlist on the strength of its careful user agency and an unusually candid prototype presentation. In a fictional family-pressure scenario, the film shows a private reflection flow, a user correcting an interpretation, a proposed community match with its basis exposed, and an exact audience/text preview before sharing. It repeatedly labels responses and people as prepared or illustrative, and plainly states that no AI service or real community is connected. Reservations: a curated screen tour, no completed support outcome, small UI text, and inaccessible audio.

## First 15 seconds

At 0:00, “A place to begin, at your pace” offers private talk, peer stories, or supporting others, with no account needed. The top bar labels “Illustrative scenario · fictional participants.” A quote reads, “I want to hear what others went through, without anyone deciding for me.” The subtitle at 0:00 frames the need: “When family expectations make it hard to know what you want, Harmonise gives you private room to think before you decide.” The promise is clear, but the audience is implied and differentiation from journaling or peer support is broad. At 0:16 the private flow clarifies that the user can “Talk it through privately, or explore at your own pace.”

## Scene audit

At 0:00–0:08, a calm landing screen offers several valid starting points and explicitly says private words are not included in community posts or the prepared response. The static opening takes several seconds to show what makes the product distinct.

At 0:16–0:40, replies are labeled “local example rules · no AI service is connected.” A prepared reply follows “I feel ungrateful asking my parents for room to think.” The film subtitle at 0:24 explicitly says no model is connected. At 0:40 the story shows the person choosing “Not quite” and editing their words, an effective demonstration of user correction instead of unquestioned system interpretation.

At 0:56, a “Thought to check” card separates “What you said,” “Might matter,” and “What you want now: Not confirmed yet,” with confirm, reject, and edit controls. This is a strong safeguard against overclaiming certainty. At about 1:12, community discovery explains a mismatch: a broad topic result about separation paperwork is set aside in favor of confirmed context about family expectations and personal choices. The screen says “No match scores—just the reasons behind this illustrative comparison.” The selection is legible without false precision.

At 1:28, “Ask Ravi in the group” previews audience, alias, and question, excluding private conversation and direct messages. The subtitle notes consent remains unchecked. Around 2:00, the next community scene labels posts as prepared historical examples, lets the user compose a bounded reply, and previews the exact group, alias, and text. At 2:48, consent is still unchecked; the control says “Post in this example group,” and the screen states nothing is sent to a real group. The film does not show what happens after sharing, declining, or needing help. An editorial card at 2:32 labels planned Meta roles as disconnected, interrupting the journey.

## Authenticity, attribution, accessibility, and craft

The film labels participants fictional, chat replies prepared local examples, community posts historical examples, and Meta roles disconnected. Apparent community activity is therefore illustrative, not evidence of live people, network effects, or model output. The polished path is linear and curated. No running-model dialogue is substantiated. The proposal names Muse Spark for dialogue/correctable context and optional Muse Voice Transcribe with review. These roles plausibly support reflection and accessibility, but execution is not evidenced. The calm, consistent interface sits in a small central area, with tiny body copy. Captions are readable; no disruptive motion is visible. With audio inaccessible, vocal delivery, music, and actual audio pacing cannot be judged.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:00–0:15 — Audience and differentiation are implied.** Add a line naming the intended user and why the private-to-peer path matters. **Expected:** viewers name user and promise. **Verify:** ask a first-time viewer at 0:10.
2. **0:40–0:56 — Correction uses a prepared exchange.** Show revised understanding replacing the prior reading after “Not quite.” **Expected:** correction feels meaningful. **Verify:** both readings are legible across the change.
3. **1:12 — Match rationale is compact.** Enlarge the confirmed context and rejected broad topic; retain “no match scores.” **Expected:** viewers understand the result’s basis. **Verify:** text reads at playback size.
4. **1:28–2:48 — Sharing safety is carefully previewed but the path is long.** Compress repeated community reading and retain the exact audience/text review, unchecked consent, and discard option. **Expected result:** the safety mechanism remains memorable without stalling the story. **Verify:** the user sees scope, explicit consent, and a safe exit in one uninterrupted beat.
5. **About 2:32 — Planned roles interrupt the journey.** Put the proposed/disconnected labels beside the relevant flow or in a brief end card after a human outcome. **Expected result:** architecture stays candid while the story reaches a payoff. **Verify:** viewers can distinguish proposed Meta roles from demonstrated prototype behavior.

## Judge reactions and release recommendation

**Skeptical objection:** This is a thoughtfully designed interaction storyboard, not evidence that a model can understand a sensitive family situation safely or that a community can deliver reliable support. The prepared replies, fictional people, and local example rules avoid the hardest test: a real user saying something ambiguous, correcting the system, and receiving a safe, useful next step. There is no observed live AI, live group, or completed support outcome.

**Best-case reaction:** The film treats agency and privacy as product behavior. It lets a person revise the system’s reading, exposes why a community result appears, and asks for review of the exact audience and words before any example share. Its candid labels make the prototype’s boundaries unusually easy to trust.

**Release recommendation:** Shortlist for the design and interaction quality shown in this film. The explicit blocker to any claim of deployed AI or active community support is the film’s own disclosure that responses are prepared local examples, participants are fictional, and Meta integrations are disconnected. A later release film should show the intended real execution truthfully and preserve the clear correction, scope preview, consent, and exit controls.
