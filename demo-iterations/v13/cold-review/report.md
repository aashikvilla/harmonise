# Cold film review — v13

## Executive decision: Maybe

The film takes a specific problem—an adult unsure how to answer family pressure—and shows a bounded path that protects agency. It clearly labels fictional, prepared, and unconnected content. I hold at “maybe” because most of the 2:59 tours dense interface states: safeguards are clearer than a live product experience or network outcome.

Three reasons:

1. Arjun’s correction changes the proposed reflection into his own words, and the film repeatedly leaves the decision with him (about 0:10–0:25; 2:35–2:58).
2. Sharing scope is concrete and consent remains unchecked, with no-send and leave paths visible (about 1:30–1:45; 2:15–2:58).
3. Prepared examples dominate the film, while no model call, real participant, service, or outcome is shown; the narration/captions acknowledge this, but the experience is consequently more design proposal than demonstrated product (throughout, especially 0:45, 1:40, 2:40).

First-15-second clarity: 20/25.

## Scene audit

At 0:00–0:08 the film establishes the conflict and labels the scenario fictional; the opening screen is small within substantial whitespace. At about 0:10–0:20, Arjun can accept, reject, or edit an interpretation, then the wording changes to his own. This is the clearest interaction and agency signal. The AI is labeled planned and not connected.

At 0:25–0:45 it moves to prepared community examples, including an unresolved question and host boundary: “I can listen; I can’t decide for you.” Captions identify these as fictional, not live. At 0:55–1:15 an example question is edited and an audience/alias preview appears. At 1:30–1:45, private context is excluded, consent is unchecked, and “Continue” is disabled. At 1:45–2:00 no provider is verified or bookable. From 2:05 to the end, a private takeaway sits beside an optional group draft; the audience and alias remain visible, consent stays unchecked, and the person keeps the next step. The safeguards are strong, but repeated screens and small text slow the last half.

Missed inference: the film does not show how real users reach the flow or how peers/support are found. Captions mostly prevent viewers from mistaking examples for real outcomes.

## Accessibility and film craft

Burned-in captions are high contrast, readable, and carry key caveats. UI text is smaller and dense, especially on community and sharing screens, which occupy part of the frame. Restrained navigation helps comprehension but makes the middle repetitive. The emotional movement ends quietly with a bounded next step. Audio could not be assessed.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:00–0:15 — Clarify the product promise earlier.** Replace the small overview card with a concise opening sentence and a larger demonstration of “reflect privately, correct the framing, choose whether to share.” Expected result: viewers can state the product’s value by 15 seconds. Verify with a cold viewer after a 15-second cut.
2. **0:25–1:15 — Make the illustrative status unmistakable at the moment examples appear.** Hold a large “prepared example — no real participants” label over the thread and its day-by-day cards. Expected result: viewers do not infer live community or actual replies. Verify by asking viewers what evidence of live activity they saw.
3. **0:55–1:30 — Enlarge the exact sharing preview.** Crop to the question, alias, audience, and exclusion of private context instead of showing the full page at once. Expected result: consent scope is readable without pausing. Verify from a normal-size playback.
4. **1:30–2:10 — Compress repeated privacy and provider screens into a purposeful branch.** Show one decision point and its decline/return outcome, keeping “no verified provider” visible. Expected result: the middle advances the story while preserving safeguards. Verify with a timed cut and viewer recall of the available exit.
5. **2:15–2:58 — Land the ending on the person’s choice.** End on the private takeaway beside the optional draft, with unchecked consent and disabled posting held long enough to read. Expected result: a memorable, truthful payoff centered on agency. Verify that viewers can describe what was and was not sent.

## Judge reactions

**Skeptical objection:** “This is a thoughtful storyboard for a sensitive product, but the film mostly tours static prepared screens. I cannot tell whether the core interpretation is produced by a model, whether any community can help, or whether a suitable professional exists. The disclosed preview status is honest, yet the entry has not demonstrated its central service.”

**Best-case reaction:** “The team understands that support in a family decision should not become pressure from an AI, peers, or a directory. Arjun can correct the language, keep the reflection private, and decide later whether to share a carefully scoped draft. The film makes those boundaries concrete and refuses to fabricate live use.”

## Release recommendation and blockers

Recommend **maybe**, if prototype-centered films are eligible. No working model, live peer exchange, verified provider, or real user outcome is shown; this is disclosed, but limits execution and impact claims. A shorter, more legible cut showing one complete consent/decline branch would improve the film.
