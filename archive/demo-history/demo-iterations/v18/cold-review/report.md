## Executive decision: Maybe

Harmonise addresses a concrete, emotionally credible situation: an adult feels pressed by family to decide something before they are ready. The film shows a respectful flow where the person corrects an interpretation, keeps a next step private, and reviews the exact scope of later sharing. It is unusually candid that the chat, community stories, replies, and provider directory are prepared or fictional, with no model, live person, or send action connected. That honesty builds trust, but leaves the product as a convincing prototype rather than a functioning AI or community service. I would keep it in contention for a concept/prototype track, with a tighter film and clearer AI value.

## Authenticity, Meta use, accessibility, and craft

The film is linear and staged, but says so. It labels the scenario fictional, posts prepared historical examples, and repeatedly states that no model, live person, moderator, or send action is connected. The transformation from vague pressure to “I can ask for time before deciding” is modest and believable. Muse Spark is proposed to organize a correctable reflection; optional voice transcription is also planned. Neither is substantiated as a live call. The film supports the interaction pattern, not AI execution.

Burned captions are high contrast and readable. Many interface labels may be small at presentation distance. Screen transitions are easy to follow but static. The opening is emotional; the ending is quiet, with little visual variation. Speech clarity, music, vocal pacing, and caption synchronization are unjudged; captions provide a usable narration path.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **00:00–00:10 — State the distinction.** Add one short line that the person can correct the reflection and choose what leaves private space. This should make the product legible beyond chat or journaling. Later verify by asking first-time viewers to name the distinction after 15 seconds.
2. **00:20–00:45 — Center the correction moment.** Hold on the original words, edited words, and chosen next step; reduce competing page chrome. Verify that viewers can say what Arjun changed and chose.
3. **00:50–01:15 — Clarify community status and role.** Introduce the prepared peer timeline concisely, then emphasize the host boundary and unanswered question. Verify that viewers distinguish historical examples from live posts.
4. **01:20–02:40 — Condense the sharing/support tour.** Keep the exact audience preview, one cancel/later branch, and unavailable-provider disclosure; shorten repeated page views. Verify that viewers can state what is shared and what stays private.
5. **02:40–02:56 — End on one memorable choice.** Finish on the private takeaway beside the unsent draft, with “Nothing is sent” visible and a short invitation to continue when ready. Verify that viewers recall both the optional next step and that nothing was sent.

## Judge reactions

**Skeptical objection:** This is a thoughtful interface concept with prepared dialogue and fictional community posts. Its consent and correction pattern is strongest, but model need, live peers, and qualified help are unproven. At nearly three minutes, repeated pages ask for more attention than the evidence warrants.

**Best-case reaction:** The product keeps a sensitive decision with the person making it. Arjun can reject the framing, edit his words, keep a thought private, inspect an audience, and leave without sending anything. Its candid, quiet outcome feels safer than a forced resolution.

## Release recommendation

**Release as a concept/prototype submission only.** The explicit disclosures block any claim of live AI, peer activity, moderation, provider verification, or messaging. Improve the competition film by shortening the sharing/support section and sharpening the opening and correction branch. This MP4 does not establish live model execution or a production service.
