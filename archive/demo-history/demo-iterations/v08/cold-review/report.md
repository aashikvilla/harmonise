**Evidence boundary:** I reviewed only the candidate MP4 and the supplied video-only rubric. The film is 176.8 seconds at 1920×1080 and contains an audio stream. Audio playback/transcription were unavailable, so I used visible interface text and burned-in captions only. I did not infer uncaptained narration. Audio-dependent judgments have reduced confidence.

## Executive decision: Maybe

The film earns a maybe through its clear audience, emotionally credible example, and unusually concrete privacy and consent controls. It shows a tentative reflection being corrected, then a share flow that previews the exact audience and text. Its social thread allows disagreement and an unanswered question to remain unresolved. The central limitation is also plainly disclosed: the participants and community activity are fictional/prepared, the AI is not connected, nothing is sent, and no provider directory is connected. This is a thoughtful prototype film, not evidence of a running service or AI.

## Authenticity and credibility

Arjun is labeled fictional; assistant replies use prepared examples/local rules; the multi-day discussion is prepared historical content. The film is a selected, linear demonstration, not an unscripted session. Its repeated labels make that clear. The fictional host/moderator does not establish live community safety. The share preview and disabled send state show interface behavior, not an actual network post. The support screen warns that sample provider titles are unverified and says no directory or credential checks are connected.

## Meta model attribution

The film names **Muse Spark** as a planned aid to organize a correctable reflection and optional **Muse voice transcription** as a planned way to turn reviewed speech into text (0:08, 0:32, 0:48). A correctable reflective role fits the interaction, but the film says local prepared rules are active and no model is connected. It substantiates a proposed role and interface design, not a model call, reasoning, transcription, or generated response. Model necessity is plausible but not established against simpler approaches.

## Accessibility and film craft

Burned-in captions are present and generally readable at 0:00, 0:24, 0:48, 1:20, and 2:56. They sometimes overlay the interface; dense text is harder to scan in wider views. Motion is restrained; emotional movement comes from uncertainty, correction, and user control. The film prioritizes interface evidence over cinematic atmosphere. Sound design and spoken performance cannot be judged.

## Five highest-impact fix tickets

1. **0:00–0:15 — Differentiation comes late.** Preview the “A thought to check” controls while retaining the fictional/no-AI notice. Expected: immediate understanding of the distinctive mechanism. Verify with a cold viewer asked what the product does after 15 seconds.
2. **0:24–0:56 — Prepared interaction could be mistaken for AI execution.** Keep a prominent “prepared local example · no model connected” label on the correction sequence. Expected: clear separation of prototype evidence and planned model role. Verify viewers can identify which is which.
3. **1:04–1:44 — Community story is dense.** Focus on one peer reply, the host boundary, unanswered side question, and “no report was sent.” Expected: peer value and moderation limits legible at film pace. Verify with a pause-frame readability check.
4. **2:32–2:56 — Ending stops at a form.** Close on disabled consent/post controls, then a full product view with a short user-control takeaway. Expected: memorable conclusion without implying a real post. Verify that the last five seconds communicate both the choice and that nothing was sent.
5. **Throughout — Audio contribution is inaccessible in this review.** Ensure captions carry every substantive narration claim and interaction change, clear of controls. Expected: story works muted. Verify by reviewing sound off and checking all claims against captions.

## Skeptical judge objection

“This is a thoughtful UI prototype, but the impressive AI and social outcomes are prepared examples. I can see intended safeguards; I cannot see a live model helping anyone, peers responding, actual support access, or adoption evidence. The film risks making an ambitious service feel more complete than the disclosed prototype is.”

## Best-case reaction

“Harmonise understands that people under family pressure may need room to think before explaining themselves. Its reflection does not claim certainty, and the sharing flow makes audience, alias, exact text, and excluded context visible before consent. The unresolved thread and disabled send button show restraint rather than manufactured closure. This is a careful direction for private reflection and optional peer support.”

## Release recommendation and blockers

**Advance as a maybe, with prototype status stated plainly.** The film communicates the concept and safeguards; sharpening the opening and ending would strengthen it. Evidence-based blockers to a higher-confidence claim are explicit in the film: no AI/model connection, no live community or moderation, no actual sending, and no verified provider directory. These are limits of the presented experience, not claims about unseen systems.
